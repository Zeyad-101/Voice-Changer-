Signal path

  
Recording and playback are two separate steps. While you hold BLUE, the mic fills a buffer in RAM. When you press an effect button, the ESP32 reads that buffer, shifts the frequency and sends it to the amp in blocks of 256 samples (16 ms).

  

    
1
Mic records
While BLUE (D27) is held, the INMP441 sends 16,000 samples per second over I2S.

    
2
ESP32 stores
I2S port 0 reads pin D34 into a RAM buffer of about 3 s. Let go of BLUE and it stops.

    
3
ESP32 shifts the frequency
GREY ×1.6, WHITE ×0.65 or BLACK robot, applied to the saved recording.

    
4
Amp plays
I2S port 1 sends the new samples on D19. D5 wakes the amp only while it plays.

  


  

    

      
What “changing the frequency” means

      
Most men speak at about 85–180 Hz and most women at 165–255 Hz. Multiply every frequency in the voice by one number and the voice changes character.

      

        
×1.6chipmunk, higher

        
×0.65monster, deeper

        
×sin 50Hzrobot, ring modulation

      

      
In the simulation, hold SW1 to record, then press SW2 and watch the orange peaks in the spectrum sit to the right of where the blue ones were. SW3 moves them left.

    

    

      
Why these pins work together

      
    
        
  • Two I2S ports. The mic (in) and the amp (out) each get their own port, so both run at the same time.
    • 
        
  • D34 is input-only. Mic data only ever comes in, so this pin is a perfect fit.
    • 
        
  • D17 and D16 carry the screen SPI. The ESP32 can route its hardware SPI to almost any pin, so the code calls SPI.begin(17, -1, 16, 15) and the screen still draws at full speed.
    • 
        
  • Buttons on 27, 26, 22 and 23 use the ESP32's built-in pull-ups, so you don't need resistors.
    • 
        
  • D2, D5 and D15 are boot pins. They are safe here because the screen and amp only listen on them. If an upload fails, unplug the screen DC wire (D2) and try again.
    •

Wiring 4-leg buttons

  
A 4-leg tactile button is really a 2-contact switch. Its legs come in pairs that are always connected inside, and pressing the button joins the two pairs.

  

    

      
    

    

      
    
        
  1. Place the button across the middle gap of the breadboard.
    2. 
        
  2. Wire one diagonal leg to GND and the opposite diagonal leg to the ESP32 pin. Diagonal legs are always on different pairs, so this can't go wrong.
    3. 
        
  3. In code use pinMode(pin, INPUT_PULLUP). The pin reads HIGH when released and LOW when pressed. You don't need a resistor.
    4. 
      

      

        
ButtonPinJob

        
SW1 BlueD27Hold = record. Let go = stop

        
SW2 GreyD26Play as chipmunk ×1.6

        
SW3 WhiteD22Play as monster ×0.65

        
SW4 BlackD23Play as robot (ring modulation 50 Hz)

      

      
All four are free, safe pins with built-in pull-ups. Avoid D0 and D12 for buttons, because they affect booting.
